Primary finding

The 98% was never tested

The 2020 test set had 42 cases. 41 of them were exact copies of training cases. On top of that, only 305 of the 4,962 rows in the data were unique. The score looked great, but it measured memory, not judgement.

Wider field · 2020 to 2026

How prediction changed around it

  • Input
    Language replaced checkboxes. Since LLMs arrived, symptom checkers let you describe how you feel in your own words, instead of picking from a fixed list.
  • Output
    Ranked answers, with doubt. Good tools now show several options with how sure they are, and say when they don't know.
  • Evaluation
    Leakage checks became basic hygiene. Removing duplicates, testing on unseen data and publishing model cards are now the minimum for any claimed score.
  • Regulation
    Health AI became high-risk by law. The EU AI Act puts medical AI in its high-risk class, and regulators such as the FDA publish guidance for AI in medical devices.
  • Shipping
    Deploying became one click. Free hosts like Streamlit Community Cloud replaced hand-built Heroku setups, whose free tier ended in 2022.
Discharge summary

What I learned

  • Data quality beats model choice. A score is only as good as the test behind it. Mine was measured on copies of the training data.
  • Ship it, and make it reproducible. A training script that crashes and a "coming soon" deploy are not a finished project.
  • Health AI must be humble. Show uncertainty, show alternatives, and never pose as a doctor.
  • LLMs changed how I build. Today I'd start from language. Predico stays classic ML on purpose: small, explainable, free to run and no API key needed.
